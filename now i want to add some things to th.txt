What problem you’re solving
Telegram Mini Apps cannot load http://localhost:5173 on your phone. Telegram needs a public HTTPS URL.

So you run the Mini App locally, then put a tunnel in front of it:

Phone (Telegram) → https://public-tunnel-url → your PC → localhost:5173
The bot stores that public URL in .env as TELEGRAM_MINI_APP_URL, and buttons open that URL as a Web App (not a normal website link).

Why Telegram login failed (the real bug)
When Telegram opens a Mini App correctly, it attaches secret login data in the URL hash, like:

https://your-tunnel-url/#tgWebAppData=...&tgWebAppVersion=...
Your app reads that as initData and logs the customer in.

If that hash is missing, you get:

Telegram login data is missing (initData empty)

So the page can still show “Kings Cut”, but you are not signed in.

Why free ngrok failed
Free ngrok often shows an interstitial warning page (“Visit Site”) before your app.

What happens:

Telegram opens:
https://xxx.ngrok-free.dev/#tgWebAppData=...
ngrok intercepts and shows its warning page (or a redirect)
After that, the page loads without #tgWebAppData
initData is empty → auth screen
Also, every time you restart free ngrok, the URL changes, so you must update .env + BotFather + restart the bot again.

Ngrok itself isn’t “bad”; the free warning page breaks Mini App auth.

Why Cloudflare Tunnel works
cloudflared tunnel --url http://localhost:5173 gives a temporary URL like:

https://something.trycloudflare.com
It usually:

serves your app directly (no interstitial)
keeps Telegram’s #tgWebAppData=... hash
so initData works and login succeeds
Same idea as ngrok (public HTTPS → localhost), but without the free-plan warning page that strips auth.
